Overview ​

Some flows can't be built directly from an optimal control problem — the control law itself has to be derived first, for example a singular control on an arc where the usual maximization condition degenerates. Deriving it needs differential-geometry tools: Lie derivatives, Lie brackets, Poisson brackets. This section is that toolkit. It moved to its own package, CTLie, in v2.1.0-beta.

julia
using OptimalControl

What this is for ​

  • Computing a singular control — the standard chain of iterated Poisson brackets (, , , ...) that gives on a singular arc.

  • Checking controllability via the Lie brackets of the system's vector fields.

  • Building a Hamiltonian from a vector field — the canonical lift used throughout the indirect-methods section.

Two vocabularies ​

Two kinds of objects appear throughout: vector fields live on the state space (  ), Hamiltonians live on the cotangent space (  ). ad and its bracket operate on the first vocabulary, Poisson on the second. Lift is the bridge from one to the other.

The bridge identity ​

Lifting turns a Lie bracket into a Poisson bracket:  , i.e. Poisson(Lift(X), Lift(Y)) ≈ Lift(ad(X, Y)). It is the single best check that the two halves of the toolkit agree — not a linear example, where the bracket is trivially zero:

julia
X(x) = [x[1]^2, x[2]^2]
Y(x) = [x[2], -x[1]]

lhs = Poisson(Lift(X), Lift(Y))
rhs = Lift(ad(X, Y))

x, p = [1.0, 2.0], [3.0, 4.0]
lhs(x, p), rhs(x, p)
(12.0, 12.0)

Autonomous, non-autonomous, variable ​

Every operation here takes is_autonomous::Bool and is_variable::Bool keywords (default true/false — time-independent, no extra parameter). Operands that disagree — one autonomous, one not, say — throw a PreconditionError naming both traits explicitly; see Lie derivative and Lie bracket for the exact message.

Automatic differentiation ​

Everything here is AD-backed except Lift, which is a purely algebraic rearrangement (  , no derivative involved). See AD backend for how the derivatives themselves are computed and how to change the backend.
